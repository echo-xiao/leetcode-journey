Practice in the browser, read on your phone

The Chrome extension handles practice and records it automatically; it does not push new problems at you by default. The iOS app is read-only and handles the overview, the reading and the reminders. The problem list is read straight from a GitHub repo. The page keeps the original spec's way of presenting things. Interface and all three share cards follow the flomo screenshots: dark ground, low-contrast cards, body 16 / leading 28, card padding 17, radius 16.

Targets live in the extension, not the app

Not a preference, an architectural consequence: the app asks for read scope only and cannot write anything. Any setting that changes scheduling has to live on the end that can write. Chrome sets the target, the app keeps score.

Scope can intersect without touching scheduling

Company, tag and list are picked on a searchable multi-select page; the home screen keeps only what you chose and folds anything past two into +N. Within a group the terms are OR'd, across groups AND'd. Done applies, Back or Esc discards this round, and the other dimensions stay put.

Why a side panel and not a popup

A popup closes the moment you click elsewhere -- flomo's own extension does exactly this. A side panel stays open and follows you from problem to problem, which is the only way it can be there for the whole session.

Why nothing is injected into the DOM

Injecting means guessing LeetCode's markup, and you go blind the moment they ship a redesign. The side panel is a region the browser hands you, and it reads from GraphQL only. LeetHub has been forked four times for precisely this reason.

Completion is recorded, not rated

A fresh accepted submission on a queued problem records the session as done. Ladder mode advances along the preset intervals, Rounds mode by whole-round progress. Reading on the phone never counts as completion.

Why the answer starts collapsed

See the answer on open and spaced repetition is over before it began. Collapsing is not about saving space, it is about forcing one genuine attempt at recall.

Why it opens in two stages

Pseudocode and notes are hints at different depths. Recording which layer you stopped at carries far more signal than a bare did-you-peek flag.

Degraded states get said out loud

No network, expired token, LeetCode session dropped -- the status dot in the title bar changes color and an actionable amber bar appears under the body. The data queues locally and is not lost, but you have to be able to see that it has not been pushed yet.

iOSHome is a drawer

Same as flomo on phone: what you normally see is the reading page. Tap ☰ at the top left and the navigation opens to 79% of the screen, pushing the reading page right and leaving a rounded sliver of it visible.Pick a category and the drawer closes itself, leaving the problems in that category on screen.

The drawer holds three large numbers, the heatmap, today's progress, and List / Tag / Company / Time as four dimensions, with a horizontal switcher and matching list. On the right of each row sits due / total. No cards anywhere in this flow.

Cards are summoned, not folded away

The next card does not exist until you tap — this is the original ChainCardView mechanic (opened as far as revealed). There is never a greyed-out placeholder row on screen.

The first layer gives the problem, not the solution

Knowing what is being asked while still having to work out how is where review actually starts. The problem statement is a sentence or two in your own words: clean on copyright, available offline, and the retelling is itself the test of whether you understood it.

Notes are review.md

There is no separate notes file. What you already wrote under 'retrospective' was always how to think about this problem and where you got stuck. The interface calls it Notes; the filename does not move. The ==marked== amber highlights inside are the most expensive thing in this dataset.

The heatmap talks to LeetCode directly

It bypasses the repo and refetches every time the app comes to the foreground. Offline it draws the last result and labels it 'as of DD Mon' — it must never draw empty, because empty reads as 'you did not practice'.

No rating buttons

Problems you leafed through on the phone do not feed scheduling. That is the price of having exactly one scheduler in the system, and it is paid willingly.

The numbers are due / total

Not a progress bar. The green one is what is waiting for you right now, the grey one is how many exist in that category. It reuses the existing HomeEntry backlog / total.

ShareThree cards, three kinds of record

A span of days, kept as three numbers and a heatmap; a problem you finally understood, shared with its title, link and your own notes; the end of a day, listed as what you worked on. All three images use flomo's dark card, type sizes, spacing and low-saturation green.

Four columns of submissions · one row per problem · flomo type and spacing

A span of days

Three numbers laid out as EASY / MEDIUM / HARD. The numerator counts distinct problems accepted in the window, the denominator distinct problems attempted in it. Heatmap, dates and numbers all use the same window; lifetime totals never leak in.

Single-problem notes

Number, title, difficulty, problem link and your own notes make one image. Code can ride along with the notes; long notes extend with the content rather than truncating the part that matters. The link in the image is text, and a clickable one can be attached alongside.

Daily problem list

The same four columns as the submissions table: last submission, problem and difficulty, last result, attempts. One row per problem with alternating row tints; long titles ellipsize and reveal in full on hover. The arrows here are image styling only.

Everything above is sample data from the spec. All three shares are user-initiated; this page shows styling only and is not wired to real submissions.

REMOVEDScreens that no longer exist

The ones that lost their reason to exist once the app became read-only. Not one value in the visual spec moves.

ComponentReason
SessionViewA session is the container for a rating flow. No rating, no session — only how far you read
GradeBarRating is gone; the extension records completion when it sees an accepted submission
SessionRunner / SessionBuilderAs above
FSRS.swift / GradingScheduling runs in exactly one place, the extension: Ladder or Rounds. The app only reads due dates to schedule local notifications
Session length in SettingsViewThe app's only knob, and it goes with the session
WidgetExplicitly not doing this. An amplifier is not an engine — it amplifies a habit that already exists, it cannot create one. Addable later at zero architectural cost

HomeView is not removed, it is rebuilt. What used to be one row per session-startable scope becomes the drawer: counters + heatmap + today + four dimensions of # list. Tapping into a row still gives you the bead-reading experience, untouched.
